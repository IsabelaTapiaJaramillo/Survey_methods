# Scale documentation — Identification as Galapagueño/a (ID)

*Status: **adapted** measure (no scale for social identification appears in the Step 03 table; see `../notes.md`). Wording reviewed and approved by the author, 2026-11-10.*

- **Construct name:** In-group identification with the local community of Galápagos residents ("Galapagueño/a"), the social-identity component of SIMPEA (Fritsche et al., 2018).
- **Source / citation:** Adapted from the hierarchical multicomponent model of in-group identification (Leach et al., 2008, *JPSP*, *95*(1), 144–165). I use **one item from each of four components** (solidarity, satisfaction, centrality, individual self-stereotyping) to keep the survey short while still covering the breadth of the construct.
- **Items (numbered):** The target group "[in-group]" is replaced with "Galapagueños/as".

| # | Variable | Leach et al. component | English | Spanish draft |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ID1 | `id_solid` | Solidarity | I feel a bond with other Galapagueños. | Siento un lazo con los demás galapagueños. |
| ID2 | `id_satis` | Satisfaction | I am glad to be Galapagueño/a. | Me alegra ser galapagueño/a. |
| ID3 | `id_central` | Centrality | Being Galapagueño/a is an important part of how I see myself. | Ser galapagueño/a es una parte importante de cómo me veo a mí mismo/a. |
| ID4 | `id_selfster` | Individual self-stereotyping | I have a lot in common with the average Galapagueño/a. | Tengo mucho en común con el galapagueño/a promedio. |

- **Response format:** 5-point Likert (1 = Strongly disagree … 5 = Strongly agree), the same as the other attitude blocks, to reduce respondent burden. Leach et al. used a 7-point scale. I chose 5 points for consistency across face-to-face blocks and record the change as an adaptation.
- **Scoring rules:** Mean of ID1–ID4 (range 1–5), computed if at least 3 of 4 items are answered.
- **Known reliability:** Johnson and Reimer (2023) used three Leach et al. solidarity items ("I feel a bond with / solidarity with / committed to people in my community") and found α = .81 at the community level. Leach et al. (2008) reported adequate-to-high internal consistency for each component across several samples and groups. I will cite the exact coefficients for these items from the source tables in the final draft. This four-item composite has not been validated in Ecuador, so the **pilot is the first reliability test** (α and ω; target ≥ .70). I will also check that a one-factor CFA fits adequately.
- **Validation notes:** Leach et al. (2008) show that solidarity, satisfaction and centrality load on a higher-order *self-investment* factor and self-stereotyping on *self-definition*. Using one item per component trades subscale precision for breadth and brevity. A **single-item check** (*"Me identifico como galapagueño/a"*, Postmes et al., 2013 style) can be added in the pilot to assess convergent validity.
- **Fairness / cross-group equivalence considerations:**
  - "Galapagueño/a" is a **contested category**. Mainland-born residents (often with decades on the islands) may or may not claim it, and the 1998 Special Law created formal residency categories. Cognitive interviews (8–15) will probe who people think counts as Galapagueño/a, and an *origin* variable is always modeled alongside ID.
  - For mainland-born respondents, if cognitive interviews show the label excludes them, the instruction text will say *"personas que viven en Galápagos"* as an alternative wording, and a **wording-version flag** will be recorded.
  - I will test invariance across origin (island-born vs. mainland-born) before comparing means.
- **Role in this study:** Predictor and moderator (H1c: ID × in-group norms; H2d: festival effect × ID). Measured at baseline only; it is treated as fairly stable over 6–10 weeks.
