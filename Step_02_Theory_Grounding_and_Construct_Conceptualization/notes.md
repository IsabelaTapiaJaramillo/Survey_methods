# Theory and Measurement Notes

*Short memo describing how theory informs measurement in this study.*

**Study:** Festival de las Tortugas Gigantes: residents' participation in giant tortoise conservation, Santa Cruz Island, Galápagos (ESPM 231, Assignment 2, Appendix A)
**Author:** Isabela Tapia-Jaramillo

---

## 1. The problem, in theoretical terms

On Santa Cruz, participation in giant tortoise conservation is shaped by three kinds of relationships: residents' relationships **with nature** (relational values), **with their groups** (identification as Galapagueño/a, in-group norms, collective efficacy) and **with conservation institutions** (trust in the Galápagos National Park Directorate, GNPD). Some residents care about tortoises but distrust the institution. Others face practical barriers: work schedules, family care, transport, cost. **Low participation should therefore not be read as a lack of concern.** The theory has to separate *caring* from *acting with others through an institution*.

## 2. Why SIMPEA, and not the Theory of Planned Behavior

The **Social Identity Model of Pro-Environmental Action** (SIMPEA; Fritsche et al., 2018) builds on Social Identity Theory (Tajfel & Turner, 1979). It holds that people act on environmental problems *as group members*. Three group-level processes drive action:

1. **Social identification**: how much a person defines themself as a member of the group (here, Galapagueño/a).
2. **In-group norms**: what the group is seen to value and do. Identification leads to action **only when the in-group's norms support it**. This is the key moderation.
3. **Collective efficacy**: the belief that "we, together," can make a difference.

I chose SIMPEA over the **TPB** because participation here is *collective*: community monitoring days and festivals are group events, and the real tensions are *between groups* (residents vs. the park authority; island-born vs. mainland-born residents). The TPB treats behavior as an individual attitude–norm–control calculation. It cannot represent identity, intergroup distrust, or the idea that a group's norms switch identification "on." I keep the TPB's useful insight about control by measuring **practical barriers as covariates**, similar to perceived behavioral control.

## 3. Two extensions

- **Trust in conservation institutions** (Stern & Coleman, 2015). Trust in the GNPD has three types:
  - **competence** (rational trust: the GNPD can do the job);
  - **personal relationships** (affinitive trust: GNPD staff care about and listen to residents);
  - **rules and procedures** (procedural trust: rules are fair and decisions are transparent).

  Participation in tortoise conservation usually runs *through* the GNPD, so low trust can block action even when identity, norms and values are strong.
- **Relational values** (Chan et al., 2016): values about the *relationships* between people and nature (kinship, responsibility, identity, care), not only nature's intrinsic or instrumental worth. I treat them as a **stable characteristic** measured once at baseline (and in the new post-festival sample). They are a predictor and moderator, not an outcome expected to change in six weeks.

## 4. How the festival acts on the theory

The *Festival de las Tortugas Gigantes* is designed to act on each SIMPEA lever:

| Festival element | SIMPEA lever | Measured outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Tortoises presented as part of local identity (food, music, stories, co-organized with Cooking Sections and local partners) | **Identification**: makes the tortoise part of "who we are" | Identification is measured at baseline as a moderator (H2d) |
| Neighbors publicly celebrating tortoises | **In-group norms**: "people like us care about tortoises" | Perceived in-group norms, before and after (H2b) |
| Portrait exhibition showing residents as tortoise protectors | **Collective efficacy**: "people like us *do* protect tortoises" | Collective efficacy, before and after (H2b) |
| GNPD and the Municipality as co-organizers, present in person | **Trust** (personal relationships) | Trust, before and after (descriptive; moderator in H2d) |

## 6. Hypotheses (as pre-registered)

- **H1a.** Residents with stronger relational values will report stronger intentions to participate in tortoise conservation.
- **H1b.** Trust in conservation institutions will predict participation intentions beyond relational values. Residents with strong relational values and low trust will report weaker intentions than those with strong relational values and high trust (RV × trust).
- **H1c.** Identification as Galapagueño/a will predict participation intentions more strongly when perceived in-group norms support tortoise conservation (identification × norms; the core SIMPEA moderation).
- **H2a.** Residents who receive a personal invitation will be more likely to attend the festival.
- **H2b.** Participation intentions, perceived in-group norms and collective efficacy will increase more among residents who attend than among those who do not, controlling for their scores before the festival.
- **H2c.** Attendees will be more likely to sign up for and attend the community monitoring day.
- **H2d (exploratory).** The festival's effect will be stronger among residents who strongly identify as Galapagueño/a, and weaker among those with low trust in conservation institutions.

## 7. Measurement implications drawn from the theory

1. **Separate caring from acting.** Relational values (caring) and participation (acting) are measured separately, and behavior is observed directly (Step 07), so that low participation is not misread as low concern.
2. **Measure moderators before the treatment.** Identification and relational values are measured at baseline, so they cannot be affected by the festival.
3. **Group differences are part of the theory.** Area, occupation and origin are quota variables *and* model terms (RQ1), because SIMPEA predicts that norms and identification differ across groups.
4. **Measure behavior beyond self-report.** Intentions overstate action, so festival attendance and the monitoring day provide real, costly choices (unpaid time) to test whether intention leads to behavior.
